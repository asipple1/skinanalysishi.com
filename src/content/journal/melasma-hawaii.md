Melasma shows up as patches of brown or gray-brown pigment, usually on the cheeks, forehead, and upper lip. It is common in women, often triggered by hormones, and made worse by sun and heat. In Hawaiʻi, where UV levels stay high all year, it can be especially persistent.

## Why melasma comes back

Melasma is not simply a spot on the surface. The pigment-producing cells in your skin are overactive, and they respond to UV light, visible light, and even heat. That is why treatments that clear pigment quickly can backfire if they irritate the skin or if sun protection slips.

> We treat melasma as something to manage over time, not something to erase in one appointment.

## How we approach it

We usually begin with home care: a pigment-regulating product, gentle exfoliation, and a tinted mineral sunscreen that blocks visible light. After a few weeks, we may add a series of mild chemical peels. We avoid aggressive heat-based treatments for most melasma.

## What you can do every day

Wear a tinted mineral SPF 30 or higher, reapply when outdoors, and add a hat at the beach. It sounds simple, but it is the part of the plan that determines whether results last.

The most common thing we hear in a consultation isn't "I want to look different." It's "I want to look like myself, just refreshed." That distinction matters more than most people realize. Dysport is one of the most useful tools we have for that goal — but it works best when understood as part of a complete picture, not as a standalone fix for every concern.

> **Key takeaways**
> - Dysport relaxes dynamic muscles; it does not add volume or change facial structure
> - Natural results come from the right dose and placement, not just the product
> - Many clients combine neuromodulators with dermal fillers for a fuller-face approach to aging
> - The most natural outcomes start with an honest assessment of what is actually changing

## What Dysport does — and what it doesn't

Dysport is a neuromodulator — it works by temporarily blocking the nerve signal to specific facial muscles. When those muscles relax, the skin above them has a chance to smooth out. That is how it softens forehead lines, the crease between the brows (glabella), and crow's feet around the eyes.

What it does not do: add volume, lift the cheeks, change the shape of the lips, or alter facial structure in any way. Dysport is purely about muscle movement. The results are temporary and reversible — onset is typically two to five days, with full effect visible around two weeks. Duration averages three to five months, though this varies by person and treatment area.

The frozen look that most clients want to avoid is almost always a dosing issue. Too much product in the wrong area, or a uniform approach applied to a face with unique anatomy, creates that flat, over-treated appearance. Properly dosed Dysport maintains natural movement — you should still be able to raise your eyebrows, squint, and smile. The lines just ease up.

## The role of volume in facial aging

Here is something that does not always come up in aesthetic marketing: the face ages in two distinct ways at the same time.

The first is loss of elasticity. Skin produces less collagen and elastin over time, and repeated muscle movement creates lines that stay even when the face is at rest. This is the category Dysport addresses.

The second is loss of volume. Subcutaneous fat shifts and thins. Bone structure changes subtly. The temples hollow. The cheeks lose their fullness. The area under the eyes deepens. These are structural changes, and no neuromodulator affects them.

This is why many clients find that Dysport alone does not address everything they are noticing. The lines soften, but the overall tiredness or hollowness remains — because that hollowness is a volume issue, not a muscle issue. Dermal fillers (typically hyaluronic acid-based) work in that space. They restore lost volume in areas like the cheeks, temples, and mid-face.

Different parts of the face also age differently. The cheeks and temples often show volume loss earlier than the lower face. The jawline softens for different reasons than the forehead. A complete refresh often means thinking about each zone separately, rather than reaching for a single solution.

## Why natural comes from the plan, not the product

We say this a lot, and it holds up: the product is less important than the plan.

Under-treating creates its own problems. Asymmetry shows up when one area is addressed and another is not. A softened forehead with untouched crow's feet looks incomplete. Partial treatment can also make patients feel like the results are not worth the effort — because they are not seeing the full effect of what a thoughtful approach could produce.

Over-treating creates the frozen look everyone wants to avoid. But it also creates proportional imbalances. A heavily treated forehead with a fully expressive lower face reads as odd. Volume added in the wrong area for the wrong reasons looks artificial.

The skill is in the mapping. Before any injection appointment, we assess how the face moves, where the actual muscle is, where volume has been lost, and what the face looks like at rest versus in motion. That assessment determines dosing and placement for Dysport. It also surfaces questions about whether additional treatments would support what the patient is trying to achieve.

> The best aesthetic results don't come from more product. They come from understanding what is actually changing and addressing it precisely.

## What a complete approach might look like

For many clients, a full-face refresh includes more than one category of treatment.

Dysport handles the dynamic wrinkle areas — forehead lines, glabella, crow's feet. That is where it performs best, and for those specific concerns, it is hard to beat.

For volume loss, dermal fillers are the standard recommendation in aesthetic medicine. Whether that makes sense for a particular patient depends on their goals, the degree of volume loss, their anatomy, and their comfort level with injectables. That conversation happens during a consultation, not as a default add-on.

Skin quality is its own category. Neuromodulators and fillers do not address texture, tone, pigmentation, or surface concerns. Treatments like DiamondGlow and SkinPen microneedling work on the skin itself — collagen production, cell turnover, resurfacing. For clients who are seeing both structural changes and skin quality changes, layering these approaches produces the most comprehensive results.

None of this is a formula. Every patient comes in with a different face, different concerns, and different goals. The point of a consultation is to figure out what is actually worth addressing and in what order — not to work through a menu.

## Frequently asked questions

### How is Dysport different from Botox?

Both are botulinum toxin type A products, and both work by relaxing targeted facial muscles. The main practical differences are in the formulation and diffusion pattern. Dysport has a slightly wider spread, which many practitioners find useful in larger muscle groups like the forehead. The onset may be marginally faster with Dysport for some patients. Results with either product depend far more on the injector's technique than on which brand is used.

### Will I look natural after Dysport?

If it is dosed and placed correctly for your anatomy, yes. The concern about looking frozen is valid, but it is almost always the result of over-treatment — too many units, placed uniformly without accounting for how the individual face moves. We start conservatively with new patients, and we do a two-week follow-up to assess symmetry and adjust if needed.

### What is the difference between Dysport and fillers?

Dysport is a neuromodulator — it relaxes muscles to soften movement-related wrinkles. Fillers are volumizers — they restore lost fullness to areas where the face has thinned over time. They address different aspects of aging. Many patients benefit from both, but the indications are distinct. Dysport will not plump the cheeks, and fillers will not relax forehead lines.

### How do I know what I actually need?

That is exactly what a consultation is for. It is not a sales appointment. We look at your face, listen to what you are noticing, and talk through what is actually driving those changes. Some patients come in expecting to need fillers and find that Dysport addresses most of their concerns. Others come in for Dysport and discover that volume loss is a bigger factor than movement. An honest assessment first is the only way to get results that actually make sense for your face.
